Overview

Protecting your interest in property

Property transactions in Sri Lanka require careful due diligence — a clear chain of title, a current encumbrance certificate, and a properly notarised deed are essential before any transfer, gift, or mortgage. Errors at this stage can lead to costly disputes years later.

I assist clients with title investigation, deed preparation, registration, and, where disputes arise, representation in land and partition litigation.

Legal Framework

Relevant Law

  • Registration of Documents Ordinance — governs the registration of deeds and other documents affecting land.
  • Prescription Ordinance — governs the acquisition of title by long, undisturbed possession.
  • Partition Law No. 21 of 1977 — governs the partition of jointly owned land.
  • Notaries Ordinance — governs the execution and attestation of deeds by a Notary Public.
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an encumbrance certificate and why is it needed?
It is issued by the Land Registry and shows recorded dealings affecting a property over a specified period, such as mortgages and transfers — a standard due-diligence document before purchase.
How long does a title search take?
This depends on the property's history and record availability, but a thorough search typically traces title back at least 30 years.
What is prescriptive title?
Under the Prescription Ordinance, open, undisturbed, and adverse possession for the statutory period may confer title independent of a formal deed.
Do I need a notary for a deed?
Yes, deeds affecting immovable property must generally be executed before and attested by a Notary Public to be validly registered.
