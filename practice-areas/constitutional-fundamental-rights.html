Constitutional & Fundamental Rights

Fundamental rights petitions before the Supreme Court of Sri Lanka, and advice on constitutional questions arising from executive or administrative action.

Overview

Enforcing rights guaranteed by the Constitution

Chapter III of the Constitution of the Democratic Socialist Republic of Sri Lanka, Articles 12 to 17, guarantees fundamental rights including equality before the law, freedom from torture and arbitrary arrest, freedom of speech, and the right to a fair trial. Article 126 vests the Supreme Court with exclusive jurisdiction to hear and determine any question relating to the infringement, or imminent infringement, of these rights by executive or administrative action.

This is a strict-timeline area of practice — a fundamental rights application must generally be filed within one month of the alleged infringement — so early consultation is important.

What This Involves

Understanding Constitutional & Fundamental Rights Law in Sri Lanka

✓
Fundamental rights petitions — under Article 126, a person may petition the Supreme Court where a fundamental right has been infringed by executive or administrative action.
✓
Unlawful arrest and detention — arrests or detention falling outside legal authority can be challenged before the courts.
✓
Interim relief — the Supreme Court may grant urgent interim relief, such as staying an impugned order, pending final determination.
✓
Equality and non-discrimination — Article 12 guarantees equal protection of the law and prohibits discrimination on specified grounds.
✓
Freedom of expression — Article 14 protects speech, assembly, and association, subject to permitted restrictions.
✓
Constitutionality of executive action — executive or administrative decisions can be examined for consistency with the Constitution.
How It Works

The Process

01
Urgent Initial Consultation
Given the strict one-month time limit under Article 126(2), the facts and date of alleged infringement are assessed immediately.
02
Drafting the Petition
Preparation of the petition and supporting affidavits, identifying the respondents and the specific constitutional provisions engaged.
03
Filing Before the Supreme Court
The petition is filed with the Supreme Court registry, and where urgent, an application for interim relief is made simultaneously.
04
Support and Hearing
The petition proceeds through the leave-to-proceed stage and, if leave is granted, to a full hearing before the Supreme Court.
05
Determination
The Supreme Court delivers its determination, which may include declarations, compensation, or other appropriate relief.
Legal Framework

Relevant Law

  • Articles 12–17, Constitution of Sri Lanka — guarantee of fundamental rights.
  • Article 126, Constitution of Sri Lanka — exclusive jurisdiction of the Supreme Court over fundamental rights applications.
  • Article 17, Constitution of Sri Lanka — right of a person to apply to the Supreme Court for redress.
  • Supreme Court Rules — governing the procedure for filing and hearing fundamental rights applications.
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the time limit to file a fundamental rights petition?
Under Article 126(2), a petition must generally be filed within one month of the alleged infringement, subject to the court's discretion in limited circumstances.
Can I obtain interim relief while my petition is pending?
Yes, the Supreme Court may grant interim relief, such as staying an impugned order, where circumstances justify it.
Do I need a lawyer to file a fundamental rights petition?
It is not strictly mandatory, but given the strict procedural and drafting requirements before the Supreme Court, representation is strongly advisable.
What happens if the respondent is a state officer?
The relevant officer and the Attorney-General are typically named as respondents, with the Attorney-General's Department representing the State.
