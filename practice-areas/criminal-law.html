Overview

Defending your rights at every stage

Criminal proceedings in Sri Lanka move through several distinct stages — investigation, arrest, production before a Magistrate, bail, trial, and, where necessary, appeal. Each stage has strict procedural rules and time limits under the Code of Criminal Procedure Act No. 15 of 1979 and the Penal Code. I represent clients at every one of these stages, whether the matter is being heard summarily before a Magistrate or as a non-summary offence forwarded to the High Court.

Whether you are a suspect during a police investigation, an accused awaiting a bail decision, or seeking to appeal a conviction, early and informed legal representation materially affects the outcome of a criminal matter.

Legal Framework

Relevant Law

  • Code of Criminal Procedure Act No. 15 of 1979 — governs arrest, bail, trial procedure, and appeal.
  • Penal Code — defines criminal offences and prescribed penalties.
  • Bail Act No. 30 of 1997 — governs the grant and refusal of bail for specified offences.
  • Evidence Ordinance — governs the admissibility and weight of evidence at trial.
  • Prevention of Terrorism (Temporary Provisions) Act — applicable in matters charged under this legislation.
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is bail available for all offences?
No. Offences are classified as bailable or non-bailable. For non-bailable offences, bail is granted at the court's discretion having regard to the gravity of the offence, risk of absconding, and risk of interference with evidence or witnesses.
How long can police detain a suspect without producing them before a Magistrate?
As a general rule, a person arrested without a warrant must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours, excluding travel time, unless specific legislation provides for an extended period.
What is the difference between a summary and non-summary offence?
Summary offences are tried directly by the Magistrate. Non-summary offences are first inquired into by the Magistrate and, if there is sufficient material, forwarded for trial before the High Court.
Can a decision be appealed?
Yes, generally to the Court of Appeal, and in certain matters further to the Supreme Court, subject to statutory time limits.
