Family & Matrimonial Law

Matrimonial proceedings, custody, maintenance and property matters, handled with sensitivity and regard to the personal law applicable to each family.

Overview

Family matters handled with care

Family law in Sri Lanka is not a single uniform system — different personal laws apply depending on the parties involved, namely the General Law, Kandyan Law, and Tesawalamai. This affects matters such as marriage, divorce, and inheritance, and identifying the correct applicable law is often the first step in any family matter.

What This Involves

Understanding Family & Matrimonial Law in Sri Lanka

✓
Divorce — divorce may proceed on a contested or uncontested basis, under the applicable personal law.
✓
Judicial separation — separation without full divorce may be sought in appropriate circumstances.
✓
Child custody and access — custody decisions are guided primarily by the welfare of the child.
✓
Maintenance — a spouse and children may claim maintenance under the Maintenance Act No. 37 of 1999.
✓
Matrimonial property — property acquired during marriage may be subject to division or dispute.
✓
Applicable personal law — family law in Sri Lanka varies depending on whether the General Law, Kandyan Law, or Tesawalamai applies.

Legal Framework

Relevant Law

  • Marriage Registration Ordinance — governs the registration of marriages under the General Law.
  • Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance — governs matrimonial property rights under the General Law.
  • Maintenance Act No. 37 of 1999 — governs maintenance claims by spouses and children.
  • Kandyan Marriage and Divorce Act — governs marriage and divorce for parties subject to Kandyan Law.
  • Tesawalamai — the customary law applicable to Jaffna Tamils in matters of marriage and property.
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How is child custody decided?
Courts are guided primarily by the welfare of the child, considering the child's age, household stability, and, where old enough, the child's own wishes, alongside applicable personal law.
Which law of divorce applies to me?
This depends on the personal law applicable to the parties — General Law, Kandyan Law, or Tesawalamai — generally determined by domicile, ethnicity, and the law under which the marriage was contracted.
Can maintenance be claimed regardless of marital status?
Yes, the Maintenance Act No. 37 of 1999 allows a spouse and children to claim maintenance in appropriate circumstances, including in certain cases outside formal marriage.
How long does a divorce take?
Uncontested divorces on agreed grounds generally proceed faster; contested matters involving custody or property disputes can take considerably longer.
