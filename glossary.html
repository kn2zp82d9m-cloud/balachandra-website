Glossary of Legal Terms

Plain-language explanations of terms commonly used in Sri Lankan legal proceedings and documents. This glossary is for general educational purposes and does not constitute legal advice.

Professional Notice: The information on this page is published for general educational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. No solicitor–client relationship is created by viewing this page.
Affidavit
A written statement of facts, sworn or affirmed before a person authorised to administer oaths, used as evidence in court proceedings.
Appellant
The party who brings an appeal against a court's decision.
Bail
The conditional release of a person accused of an offence, pending trial or further proceedings.
Decree
A formal order of the court, typically issued in civil or matrimonial proceedings, that finally determines the rights of the parties.
Defendant
The party against whom a civil action is brought.
Encumbrance
A claim, charge, or liability (such as a mortgage) attached to a property, which may affect its transfer.
Ex parte
A proceeding or order made on the application of one party, without the other side being present or heard.
Habeas corpus
A legal remedy compelling the production of a detained person before a court, to determine whether the detention is lawful.
Indictment
A formal written accusation charging a person with a serious (non-summary) criminal offence.
Injunction
A court order requiring a party to do, or refrain from doing, a specific act.
Judgment
The court's formal decision on the matters in dispute.
Maintenance
A periodic payment ordered by a court for the support of a spouse, former spouse, or child.
Mitigation
Circumstances presented to a court, typically before sentencing, to reduce the severity of punishment.
Notary
An Attorney-at-Law specially authorised to attest and certify legal documents such as deeds.
Petitioner
The party who initiates certain types of proceedings, such as a fundamental rights application or divorce.
Plaint
The document that formally initiates a civil action in the District Court, setting out the plaintiff's claim.
Plaintiff
The party who brings a civil action.
Power of Attorney
A document by which one person authorises another to act on their behalf in legal or financial matters.
Prescription
The legal principle by which rights (such as title to property) may be acquired, or claims barred, through the passage of a specified period of time.
Prima facie
Evidence sufficient, on its face, to establish a fact or case unless rebutted.
Remand
The detention of an accused person in custody pending further court proceedings.
Respondent
The party against whom certain applications (such as a fundamental rights petition or appeal) are brought.
Sui juris
Having full legal capacity to manage one's own affairs.
