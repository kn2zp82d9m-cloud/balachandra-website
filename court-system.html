Understanding the Sri Lankan Court System

Sri Lanka's courts are structured in a hierarchy, with each level handling different types of matters. This page is a general guide to that structure, published for educational purposes and not as legal advice.

Professional Notice: The information on this page is published for general educational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. No solicitor–client relationship is created by viewing this page.
1
Magistrate's Court
The first point of contact for most criminal matters, including summary trials and preliminary inquiries into non-summary offences that may later be forwarded to the High Court.
2
District Court
Handles civil matters, including debt recovery, breach of contract, property disputes, divorce and matrimonial matters, and testamentary (inheritance) proceedings. Some family matters are heard by a District Court sitting as a Family Court.
3
High Court (Provincial High Court)
Has a dual role: original criminal jurisdiction, trying non-summary offences forwarded from the Magistrate's Court; and civil appellate jurisdiction, sitting as the Civil Appellate High Court to hear appeals from District Court and Family Court civil judgments. It also exercises appellate and revisionary jurisdiction over the Magistrate's Court, Labour Tribunals, and Agrarian Services Commissioners' Tribunals within the province.
4
Commercial High Court (Colombo)
A specialised division sitting in Colombo, with original civil jurisdiction over specified high-value commercial disputes.
5
Court of Appeal
Hears appeals from High Court criminal judgments, and exercises broader appellate, revisionary, and writ jurisdiction, including in certain matters arising from the Civil Appellate High Court.
6
Supreme Court
The highest court in Sri Lanka, with exclusive jurisdiction over fundamental rights petitions and constitutional matters, and final appellate jurisdiction.

A single legal matter can move through more than one of these courts — for example, a criminal case may begin in the Magistrate's Court and, following conviction, proceed on appeal to the Court of Appeal.
