Professional Standards

Professional Conduct — Supreme Court Rules 1988

My practice is regulated by the Supreme Court (Conduct of and Etiquette for Attorneys-at-Law) Rules 1988. These rules govern every aspect of how I work with clients and in court. The following is a summary of the key obligations I am bound by:

01
Strict Confidentiality — Rules 31–36
All information — oral or documentary — acquired from or on behalf of a client is kept in strict professional confidence. This duty continues indefinitely, even after the conclusion of the matter and after the death of the client.
02
No Conflict of Interest — Rules 6–13
I am prohibited from acting in any matter in which I have a personal interest without full disclosure and written consent from the client. I will not act where a conflict exists between the interests of two or more clients.
03
Frankness and Honesty — Rule 18
I am obliged to act with complete frankness and honesty in all advice and dealings with clients. I am further prohibited from acting in any manner detrimental or prejudicial to my client's interests.
04
Due Diligence — Rules 10, 15 & 16
I must not accept any matter unless I can attend to it with due diligence. Once retained, I am duty-bound to exercise my skill and care to the best of my ability in the best interests of the client and with due regard to the court.
05
Fees and Client Funds — Rules 27–30
I am prohibited from appropriating any client funds held in trust without the client's permission. I may reduce or waive fees in cases of genuine poverty or hardship in keeping with the best traditions of the profession.
06
Client's Right to Change Representation — Rule 25
A client has the absolute right to cease instructing me at any time. I am further obliged to preserve and safeguard all property of the client entrusted to me and to return it upon request.
