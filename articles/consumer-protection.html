Consumer Protection in Sri Lanka — Know Your Rights as a Buyer

Published August 2026 · By Balachandra Thinesh, Attorney-at-Law

Consumers in Sri Lanka are protected by specific legislation aimed at ensuring fair dealing between buyers and sellers of goods and services, alongside the general law of contract.

The Consumer Affairs Authority Act

The Consumer Affairs Authority Act No. 9 of 2003 establishes the Consumer Affairs Authority and provides a framework for protecting consumers against unfair trade practices, defective goods, overcharging, and false or misleading advertising.

Remedies for Defective Goods or Services

A consumer who receives defective goods or unsatisfactory services may be entitled to remedies such as replacement, repair, refund, or compensation, depending on the nature of the defect and the terms of the transaction. Where a warranty is given, its terms also govern what remedies are available.

Where to Complain

Consumer complaints may be lodged with the Consumer Affairs Authority, which has powers to investigate and, in appropriate cases, take enforcement action against traders. Separately, a consumer may pursue a civil claim before the courts for breach of contract or negligence.

Unfair Trade Practices

The law prohibits specific unfair practices, including false representations about the quality or characteristics of goods, and misleading pricing. A trader found to have engaged in such conduct may face regulatory penalties in addition to any civil liability to the affected consumer.

Key point: Keeping receipts, warranties, and written correspondence with a trader significantly strengthens a consumer's position, whether pursuing a complaint with the Consumer Affairs Authority or a civil claim.
