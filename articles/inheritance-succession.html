Inheritance and Succession Law in Sri Lanka — An Overview

Published August 2026 · By Balachandra Thinesh, Attorney-at-Law

Inheritance in Sri Lanka is governed by a combination of general legislation and, significantly, the personal law applicable to the deceased — meaning the same set of facts can produce different outcomes depending on which legal regime applies.

Testate Succession — Wills

A person may dispose of their property after death by a valid will, executed in accordance with the formal requirements of the law, generally in writing, signed, and properly witnessed. A valid will allows the testator to determine, within certain limits, how their estate is distributed.

Intestate Succession

Where a person dies without a valid will, their estate is distributed according to the rules of intestate succession under the applicable law. Under the General Law, the Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance sets out the order in which relatives inherit. Different rules apply under Kandyan Law and Tesawalamai.

Personal Law and Inheritance

Which set of inheritance rules applies to a person's estate generally depends on their ethnicity, domicile, and the law under which their family affairs are governed — General Law, Kandyan Law, or Tesawalamai — each of which has distinct rules on how property, particularly land, passes to heirs.

The Probate Process

Administering a deceased person's estate — whether under a will or on intestacy — generally requires testamentary proceedings before the District Court to obtain probate (where there is a will) or letters of administration (where there is not), before the estate can be formally distributed.

Key point: Identifying the correct applicable personal law is often the first and most important step in any inheritance matter, since it determines both who is entitled to inherit and how the estate must be administered.
