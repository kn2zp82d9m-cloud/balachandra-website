Rights Upon Arrest in Sri Lanka — What the Law Provides

Published March 2026 · By Balachandra Thinesh, Attorney-at-Law

Being arrested is disorienting, and most people do not know what rights they actually have at that moment. Sri Lankan law — through the Constitution and the Code of Criminal Procedure Act No. 15 of 1979 — provides a number of specific protections. Knowing them can make a real difference.

The Right to Be Informed of the Reason for Arrest

Article 13(1) of the Constitution requires that a person be informed of the reason for their arrest. This is not a mere formality — it allows the arrested person to understand the nature of the allegation and to begin preparing a response, including obtaining legal representation.

The Right to Be Produced Before a Magistrate

As a general rule, a person arrested without a warrant must be produced before the nearest Magistrate within 24 hours of arrest, excluding the time reasonably necessary for the journey. This requirement exists to prevent prolonged detention without judicial oversight, though specific legislation such as the PTA can extend this period for certain offences.

The Right to Remain Silent

An arrested person is not obliged to answer questions that may incriminate them. While statements made to certain officers may be admissible in some circumstances under specific statutory provisions, the general principle against self-incrimination remains an important protection during questioning.

The Right to Retain an Attorney

An arrested person has the right to consult and be represented by an Attorney-at-Law of their choice. Legal representation at the earliest stage — ideally before or during initial questioning — can materially affect the handling of bail applications and the overall trajectory of a case.

Key point: If you or someone you know has been arrested, contacting an Attorney-at-Law immediately — even before formal charges are laid — is the single most useful step to protect your position.

The Right to a Fair Trial

Article 13(3) guarantees a fair trial by a competent court, which encompasses the presumption of innocence, the opportunity to be heard, and access to due process throughout criminal proceedings.
