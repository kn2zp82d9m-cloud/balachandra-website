Tenancy and Landlord-Tenant Disputes in Sri Lanka

Published August 2026 · By Balachandra Thinesh, Attorney-at-Law

Disputes between landlords and tenants are among the most common civil matters in Sri Lanka, and the law governing them depends significantly on when the tenancy began and which statute applies to the premises in question.

Which Law Applies

The Rent Act No. 7 of 1972 applies to certain categories of premises, particularly older tenancies in specified areas, and provides significant statutory protection to tenants, including restrictions on eviction. Tenancies falling outside the Rent Act's scope are generally governed by the ordinary law of contract and the common law relating to leases.

Grounds for Eviction

Where the Rent Act applies, a landlord may only seek eviction on specific statutory grounds, such as default in rent payment, unauthorised subletting, or the landlord's reasonable requirement of the premises for personal use. Outside the Rent Act, eviction is generally governed by the terms of the lease agreement and applicable notice requirements.

Notice Periods

The required notice period before terminating a tenancy varies depending on the type of tenancy and the applicable law. Written notice is generally advisable, and proper procedure must be followed before a landlord can seek to recover possession through the courts.

Recovery of Possession

Where a tenant does not vacate voluntarily, a landlord must generally obtain a court order for recovery of possession — self-help eviction (such as changing locks or removing a tenant's belongings without a court order) is not lawful and may expose the landlord to liability.

Key point: Both landlords and tenants should keep clear written records — the tenancy agreement, rent receipts, and any notices given — as these are central to resolving disputes if they escalate to court.

Rent Arrears and Deposits

Recovery of unpaid rent, and disputes over the return of security deposits, are typically pursued as civil claims before the District Court where they cannot be resolved directly between the parties.
