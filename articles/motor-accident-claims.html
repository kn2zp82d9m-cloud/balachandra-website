Motor Accident Claims — Your Legal Options After a Road Traffic Accident

Published August 2026 · By Balachandra Thinesh, Attorney-at-Law

A road traffic accident often raises both immediate practical questions and, in due course, legal ones — particularly where there is injury, damage to property, or a dispute over fault. This article outlines the general legal avenues available.

Reporting the Accident

Sri Lankan law requires certain road accidents, particularly those involving injury or significant damage, to be reported to the police. The resulting police report is often an important piece of evidence in any later claim.

Claims for Damages

A person who suffers injury or property damage due to another party's negligent driving may bring a civil claim for damages, covering matters such as medical expenses, loss of income, damage to the vehicle, and pain and suffering. Such claims are generally pursued before the District Court, depending on the value of the claim.

The Role of Insurance

Motor insurance, where held by the at-fault party, often plays a central role in resolving claims, whether through a direct settlement or as part of a court process. The specific terms of the relevant insurance policy affect what can be recovered and from whom.

Criminal Consequences

Depending on the circumstances, a driver involved in an accident causing death or serious injury may also face criminal liability, separate from any civil claim for damages, under provisions of the Penal Code and the Motor Traffic Act.

Key point: Prompt action matters — preserving evidence such as photographs, witness details, and medical records early significantly strengthens a later claim, and civil claims are subject to a limitation period under the Prescription Ordinance.

Time Limits

Claims for damages arising from an accident are subject to a prescriptive period under the Prescription Ordinance, after which a claim may be time-barred. It is advisable to seek advice promptly rather than delay.
