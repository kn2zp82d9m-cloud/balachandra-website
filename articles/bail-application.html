Bail Under the Code of Criminal Procedure Act — Legal Framework and Application

Published January 2026 · By Balachandra Thinesh, Attorney-at-Law

Whether an accused person can secure release pending trial is often the single most urgent question in a criminal matter. In Sri Lanka, bail is governed principally by the Code of Criminal Procedure Act No. 15 of 1979, supplemented by the Bail Act No. 30 of 1997 for specified categories of offences.

Bailable and Non-Bailable Offences

Offences are broadly classified as bailable or non-bailable. For bailable offences, an accused generally has a right to be released on bail, subject to appropriate conditions. For non-bailable offences, the grant of bail is a matter of judicial discretion, exercised having regard to factors including the gravity of the offence, the strength of the evidence, the risk that the accused may abscond, and the risk of interference with witnesses or evidence.

Where Bail Applications Are Made

In most cases, a bail application is first made before the Magistrate's Court where the matter is being heard. Where bail is refused by the Magistrate, an application for bail — often termed a revision or bail appeal — may be made to the High Court.

Factors the Court Considers

Courts typically weigh factors such as the nature and gravity of the alleged offence, the accused's ties to the community and likelihood of appearing for trial, any previous convictions, the strength of the prosecution's case at that stage, and whether release would pose a risk to any victim or witness.

Conditions of Bail

Bail is frequently granted subject to conditions, which may include the provision of sureties, periodic reporting to a police station, restrictions on travel, or a requirement not to interfere with witnesses. Breach of these conditions can result in bail being revoked.

Key point: A well-prepared bail application — supported by clear facts about the accused's background, community ties, and the specific circumstances of the case — significantly improves the prospects of release. This is often best done with legal representation from the earliest possible stage.

Special Regimes

Certain legislation, including the Prevention of Terrorism Act, provides for different and generally more restrictive bail regimes for specified categories of offences. These matters require careful, specialised handling distinct from ordinary bail applications.
