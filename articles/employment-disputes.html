Employment Disputes in Sri Lanka — Termination, Compensation, and Your Rights

Published August 2026 · By Balachandra Thinesh, Attorney-at-Law

Employment relationships in Sri Lanka are governed by a combination of statute, the terms of the employment contract, and, where applicable, collective agreements. Disputes commonly arise around termination, compensation, and working conditions.

Termination of Employment

The Termination of Employment of Workmen (Special Provisions) Act No. 45 of 1971 restricts an employer's ability to terminate the services of certain categories of workmen without prior written consent of the employee, prior approval of the Commissioner of Labour, or valid cause such as disciplinary grounds established through proper process.

Unfair Dismissal

Where termination is found to be without valid cause or proper process, the employee may have recourse through the Labour Tribunal, which can order remedies including reinstatement or compensation.

Gratuity

The Payment of Gratuity Act No. 12 of 1983 entitles qualifying employees, generally those who have completed a minimum period of service, to a gratuity payment upon leaving employment, calculated according to a statutory formula.

Labour Tribunal Process

Employment disputes, including those relating to termination and unpaid dues, are commonly brought before a Labour Tribunal, which follows its own procedure distinct from the ordinary civil courts.

Key point: Applications to the Labour Tribunal alleging unjust termination are subject to a statutory time limit, so it is important not to delay in seeking advice after a dismissal.

Other Common Issues

Beyond termination, employment disputes frequently involve unpaid salary or overtime, disciplinary inquiries, workplace harassment, and the interpretation of employment contracts.
