PTA Act

The Prevention of Terrorism Act (PTA) — An Analysis of Sri Lanka's Most Controversial Law

Published May 2026 · By Balachandra Thinesh, Attorney-at-Law

The Prevention of Terrorism (Temporary Provisions) Act, commonly known as the PTA, has been one of the most debated pieces of legislation in Sri Lanka for decades. Enacted originally as a temporary security measure, it has remained in force for far longer than its name suggests, and continues to be the subject of domestic and international scrutiny.

What the PTA Permits

The PTA grants law enforcement authorities extended powers of arrest and detention in matters connected to acts defined as terrorism-related offences under the Act. Historically, this has included detention without formal charge for extended periods, subject to detention orders issued by the relevant authority, which departs significantly from the ordinary safeguards available under the Code of Criminal Procedure Act.

Recent Amendments

In response to sustained domestic and international criticism, successive amendments have sought to introduce additional procedural safeguards, including tighter oversight of detention orders and clearer definitions of offences. However, the core architecture of the Act — its broad detention powers and departure from ordinary criminal procedure — has largely remained intact through these changes. Anyone facing a matter under the PTA should seek up-to-date advice, as amendments and their practical application continue to evolve.

What Rights Remain Available

Even under the PTA, certain constitutional protections continue to apply. Article 11 of the Constitution — the absolute prohibition on torture and cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment — is not subject to any restriction. Persons detained under the PTA may also seek recourse through fundamental rights applications to the Supreme Court under Article 126 where treatment or process falls short of what the law permits, and habeas corpus applications remain an important remedy where the legality of detention itself is in question.

Key point: A PTA matter is a specialised and high-stakes area of criminal defence. Early legal representation — ideally from the point of arrest or detention — is critical to protecting a detainee's rights and options.

Why This Matters

Whether you are directly affected by a PTA matter or simply seeking to understand the legal landscape, it is important to recognise that this legislation operates differently from ordinary criminal law, with different timelines, different safeguards, and different avenues for redress.
