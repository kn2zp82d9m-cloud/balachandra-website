Property Transactions in Sri Lanka — Legal Requirements Under the Registration of Documents Ordinance

Published February 2026 · By Balachandra Thinesh, Attorney-at-Law

Buying or selling property in Sri Lanka involves more than agreeing on a price. A handful of legal requirements protect both buyer and seller, and skipping any of them can create serious problems years down the line. This article outlines the key legal steps involved in a typical property transaction.

1. Title Search

Before any transaction, it is standard practice to trace the chain of title through prior deeds, generally as far back as 30 years, to establish that the seller has good and marketable title to transfer. This process often uncovers issues such as unresolved co-ownership, prior mortgages, or defective earlier transfers.

2. Encumbrance Certificate

An encumbrance certificate, obtained from the relevant Land Registry, sets out the recorded dealings affecting the property over a specified period — including mortgages, prior transfers, court orders, and other registered interests. This document is a core piece of due diligence before completing a purchase.

3. The Deed

A valid deed of transfer must be executed before, and attested by, a Notary Public in accordance with the Notaries Ordinance. The deed must accurately describe the property, correctly identify the parties, and comply with formal requirements — errors here can affect the deed's validity.

4. Registration

Under the Registration of Documents Ordinance, the deed must be registered at the relevant Land Registry to be effective against third parties. Registration establishes priority between competing claims and is a critical step that should not be delayed after execution.

5. Prescriptive Title

In some cases, a person may hold land through long, open, and undisturbed possession rather than an unbroken chain of deeds. The Prescription Ordinance allows title to be acquired this way after the statutory period, but such claims require careful factual and legal assessment.

Key point: A cheap or rushed conveyancing process is one of the most common sources of long-term property disputes in Sri Lanka. Proper title search and a correctly executed, registered deed are not optional extras — they are what makes a transaction legally secure.
